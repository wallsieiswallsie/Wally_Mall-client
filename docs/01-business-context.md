## Evidence boundary

**Confirmed repository facts:** Sorong-focused Indonesian UI; sample catalog and storefront discovery; seller setup previews; no real transactions or acquisition metrics. The requested initial business market is Sorong. **Product hypotheses:** the problems and strategies below. **Future business possibilities:** revenue models and expansion. There is no research dataset, TAM/SAM/SOM estimate, revenue record, or documented customer validation in this repository.

## Business Problem

The hypothesis is that locally available supply is difficult to discover systematically. Listings spread across social media, messaging, physical storefronts, and personal networks may not form an accessible catalog. Wally Mall tests whether organizing that supply produces enough repeat usefulness to sustain a marketplace.

## Customer Problem

A buyer may not know which nearby merchant has an item, what it costs, whether it is still available, or how to contact the right seller. This creates search effort and uncertainty. A catalog can reduce the first problem; freshness and responsive merchants are needed to reduce the others.

## Seller Problem

A merchant may repeatedly answer basic product questions and depend on followers or existing relationships for visibility. Maintaining another catalog could also create work. Seller value must exceed the effort of onboarding and keeping products current.

## Product Hypothesis

If enough relevant local sellers maintain searchable product information, buyers will discover useful supply and initiate more qualified conversations. If those conversations are valuable, sellers will keep their listings current. Both sides must be tested; a polished interface alone does not establish either.

## Value Proposition

Give buyers one place to find local products and merchant context; give sellers a structured discovery surface beyond their existing audience. Initial value should be assessed through relevant discovery and seller contact, not claims of completed sales that the platform cannot observe.

## Marketplace Dynamics

Supply density matters within a category and location, not just as a total listing count. Many stale or irrelevant products could make a large catalog less useful. Early category depth may be more valuable than thin coverage everywhere. More buyers without responsive supply can erode trust; more merchants without buyer interest can make upkeep unrewarding.

## Initial Market Strategy

Constrain acquisition and operational learning to Sorong. Select initial categories based on buyer needs, reachable merchants, listing freshness, and response capacity rather than assuming all 11 prototype categories deserve equal investment. Confirm what the UI's area labels mean before using them for real service coverage.

## Supply-side Strategy — proposed experiments

Recruit a small consenting merchant cohort, help each produce a complete storefront and useful listings, and observe onboarding friction. Agree how often availability should be checked. Review sellers who stop updating: lack of time, weak demand, or poor tooling imply different product changes. Do not infer real founding sellers from fixture badges.

## Demand-side Strategy — proposed experiments

Ask local buyers to find specific items, observe their search terms and unresolved needs, and test whether relevant listings lead to contact and repeat visits. Compare category entry with keyword search. Start with owned/local distribution experiments whose cost and attribution can be measured; social reach alone is not marketplace success.

## Potential Revenue Models — hypothetical

| Model | Value to validate first | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promoted listings | Sellers receive qualified discovery | Paid exposure must be labeled and protect relevance |
| Seller subscription / premium storefront | Recurring tooling or visibility value | Adds cost before merchants see consistent benefit |
| Transaction fee | Platform can observe and support transactions | External conversations do not establish chargeable sales |
| Advertising | Sufficient relevant audience | Can distract from buyer intent and increase privacy burden |

None is implemented or recorded as a current revenue stream. No pricing or market-size numbers are assumed.

## Risks

Cold-start supply, stale stock, slow responses, misleading listings, uneven category coverage, seller upkeep cost, home-business location exposure, and difficulty attributing external sales could each undermine usefulness. Operating moderation and support may be more expensive than the prototype suggests. Search terminology can also differ between merchants and buyers.

## Assumptions to Validate

| Assumption | Evidence to collect | Decision it informs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local discovery is a recurring problem | Consenting buyer interviews and task observations | Whether to pursue the discovery proposition |
| Sellers will maintain listings | Completion, publication, and subsequent update activity | Onboarding and catalog-maintenance investment |
| Search results are relevant | Judged query set, zero-result cases, result clicks | Taxonomy and search priorities |
| Contact generates merchant value | Contact attempts and optional merchant feedback | Channel design and value proposition |
| Sorong can sustain useful density | Category/area coverage and returning visitors | Acquisition focus before any expansion |
| A revenue model is acceptable | Merchant value interviews and a limited pricing experiment | Whether and how to monetize |

Use [measurement definitions](12-analytics-and-kpis.md) before setting targets. Expansion should be evaluated only after validated seller density, buyer demand, retention, and operational capacity.
